Oh, something I made. Well... the lilac. I'll tell you about the lilac bush, because you've both seen it, it's the big one by my back porch.

When I was ten I had a little sister, Annie. She was six. She had red hair like a penny and she followed me absolutely everywhere, which drove me crazy at the time. We lived in Dayton, Ohio.

That winter Annie got sick. Scarlet fever. Back then there wasn't the medicine there is now, and she was sick a long time, and in the spring she died. I'm not going to tell you it wasn't sad, because it was the saddest thing in my whole life. Our house got very quiet.

Annie's favorite thing in the whole world was the lilac at our neighbor Mrs. Pruitt's. She'd stand under it and just breathe, with her eyes shut. So Mrs. Pruitt came over one day with a shovel and a little cutting — just a stick, really, with some roots — and she said, "June, let's plant Annie a lilac." And we did. I dug the hole myself.

For two years it didn't do much of anything. And then one spring it bloomed, all purple, and the whole yard smelled like Annie standing there with her eyes shut.

And when I moved, and then when I moved again, and then when Grandpa and I bought this house, every time I took a cutting from that lilac and planted it. So the one by the porch? That's Annie's lilac. It's seventy years old, sort of.

What I learned is... you don't stop loving somebody. The love just goes into something else. You plant it. And you take care of it. That's what I want you to know.
